The premise

Machines now produce most of the first draft. What does not yet exist is the equipment a person needs to direct them — to think, decide, and review at the speed the work now moves.

Long Horizon Research is a small laboratory in San Francisco. We build that equipment, and we study what happens when people use it.

Department A — The inquiries Three open questions
I

The surface

When machines draft, the scarce act is deciding. What is the best possible surface for a person to think, judge, and review the work of many hands — some of them not human? We treat the page itself as an unsolved instrument.

Interfaces
II

Judgment, recorded

Every edit, comment, acceptance, and reversal is a trace of one person's judgment. We study how a model learns that judgment from the collaboration itself — personalization, and reinforcement learning on real work.

Personalization · RL
III

The crowded artifact

Many people and many agents now share one artifact. Who wrote what; who approves; who holds control when the work moves at machine speed. Attribution and review are instrument problems, and we build for them.

Attribution · Control
“The bottleneck is no longer the drafting. It is the deciding.”
