The agents have arrived. The instruments have not.

Long Horizon Research is a small laboratory in San Francisco. We study how people think, decide, and review when machines do most of the drafting — and we build the instruments that keep human judgment in charge.

Every edit, every comment, every acceptance and reversal is a trace of human judgment. We think those traces are the most valuable data of the coming decade — and that almost nothing is built to honor them.

The lab exists to close that gap: to make collaboration with machines legible, attributable, and worth learning from.

01Three standing questions
Ithe surface

What is the best possible surface for a person to think, decide, and review, when machines do most of the drafting?

Not a chat log, and not a wall of diffs. A person's attention is the scarce resource in the loop; we design and test surfaces that spend it only where judgment is actually required.

IIlearning judgment

Can a model learn a person's judgment from the collaboration itself?

Edits, comments, acceptances, reversals — collected carefully, the record of working together is training signal. We study personalization and reinforcement learning on real work rather than on benchmarks.

IIIthe shared artifact

How do many people and many agents share one artifact?

Attribution, review, and control have to survive a document written by ten hands, most of them mechanical. We work out what history, blame, and consent look like at machine speed.

02Instruments

We publish what we learn, and we build what we need. The instruments are how the questions get answered.

No. I Sundial sundialhub.com →

A shared workspace where people and agents write together. Every edit is attributed to its author, human or machine; every change can be reviewed, kept, or reversed. Most of the lab's questions were first met inside Sundial — and it is where the answers get tested.
