Long Horizon Research is a lab in San Francisco studying how people and agents work together, and building the instruments that keep a person in the driver’s seat.

See the research

About the lab

Drafting got cheap. Judgment did not.

Machines now produce more text, more code and more analysis than any person can read. What is missing is not output. It is the instrument that lets one person hold all of it, see exactly what changed, and decide what stands.

Research

Three open questions

We work on the parts of human and agent collaboration that nobody has settled yet.

01

The surface

What is the best place for a person to think, decide and review, when machines are doing most of the drafting?

02

Learned judgment

Can a model learn a person’s judgment from the collaboration itself? Every edit, comment, acceptance and reversal is a trace of what someone actually wanted.

03

Many hands

How do several people and several agents share one document without anyone losing track of who did what?

Our position

Judgment is the scarce thing. Every instrument we build is a way to record it, read it back, and hand it forward.

Shared workspace

People and agents writing in the same document

Hand a section to an agent and keep reading. Every edit is attributed and every change is reviewable, so you can see precisely what was touched, line by line, and keep it or take it back.

Personalization

A model that learns one person’s taste

Preferences are easy to state and hard to state well. We are interested in whether the record of how someone works, what they accept and what they quietly reverse, describes their taste better than anything they could write down.

Long Horizon ResearchOpen

Reinforcement learning

Signal from work, not from benchmarks

A document being written by a team is dense with judgment already. We are studying how to turn edits, comments, acceptances and reversals into a reward a model can actually learn from without flattening the person behind them.
