human-agent interaction, studied on real work
instruments for the person who decides what the machine wrote is good
the agents are here; the instruments are not

Long Horizon Research is a small lab in San Francisco. Machines now do most of the drafting, and the tools people read and judge that drafting with were designed for a world where a person typed every word. That gap is the whole problem. We build the instruments for the new arrangement and we study, on real work rather than on benchmarks, what a person's judgment looks like once it is written down.

Sundial, our first instrument

Research

Surface: what is the best surface for a person to think, decide and review on when machines do most of the drafting? Which things must be on screen, which must be one keystroke away, and which should a person never be asked to read at all. A reviewer reading everything at the old pace is the bottleneck we are trying to remove without removing the reviewer.

Judgment: can a model learn a particular person's judgment from the collaboration itself? Every edit, every comment, every acceptance and every reversal is a trace of judgment, produced for free while the work happens. We treat those traces as the training signal: personalization from the record of what someone actually kept, and reinforcement learning on real work instead of on tasks written for the purpose.

Multiplayer: how do several people and several agents share one document without anyone losing track of who did what? Attribution down to the edit, review that scales past the point where a human can read every line, and the ability to undo one contributor without unpicking everyone else.

Instruments

Sundial is the first one. It is a shared workspace where people and agents write together: every edit is attributed to whoever or whatever made it, and every change can be reviewed, kept or reversed. It is the first of a series. Each instrument we build has to answer one of those questions with a thing people keep using after the novelty wears off.

Careers

Do you build tools you use yourself? Have you trained a model on messy human data and had it come out useful? Long Horizon Research is hiring researchers and engineers across personalization, reinforcement learning, and interfaces. Send a page of evidence that you finish things to team@longhorizonresearch.com. A paper is fine. So is an interface you built that other people quietly kept using after you stopped promoting it. We read the work before we read the resume, and we care more about taste than about pedigree. The team is small, the lab is in San Francisco, and what you build is in front of users the same week.
